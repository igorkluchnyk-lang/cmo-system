## 1. OEM tire sizes, load/speed ratings, pressure, FWD vs AWD, and how to read the tire

### Takeaway
A 2013 Sorento came on one of three sizes depending on trim/wheel: P235/65R17 (LX, 17"), P235/60R18 (EX and SX, 18"), and 235/55R19 (Limited/SXL, 19" chrome; sources conflict on whether some SX cars had 19"). Recommended cold pressure is about 33 psi front and rear; FWD and AWD use the same sizes. The owner should read the size off the tire sidewall and the driver's door-jamb placard before buying.

### Cited Findings
- 17" (LX): original size P235/65R17 103T; T = 118 mph (190 km/h) — [SizeMyTires 2013 Sorento](https://www.sizemytires.com/vehicle/2013/kia/sorento); BFGoodrich's dealer locator URL for the 2013 Sorento LX also encodes "P235/65R17 103T" — [BFGoodrich](https://www.bfgoodrichtires.com/auto/browse-tires/by-vehicle/2013/kia/sorento/lx/P235---65R17-103T). Note: the assignment's prompt guessed 104T; the sources found say 103T. Some aftermarket replacements in this size carry 104 (higher is acceptable; lower is not).
- 18": original size P235/60R18 102H; Firestone lists 235/60R18 H for SX, EX and LX trims with 33 psi front / 33 psi rear (example: "2013 Kia Sorento SX P235/60R18 H 33 PSI/33 PSI") — [SizeMyTires](https://www.sizemytires.com/vehicle/2013/kia/sorento); [Firestone Complete Auto Care](https://vehicle.firestonecompleteautocare.com/kia/sorento/2013/tires). Note: the assignment guessed 103H; sources found say 102H.
- An Autos.ca road test of a 2013 Sorento SX described 18" rims with 235/60R18 tires; Kia's 2013 materials list "mirror-finish 10-spoke 18-inch alloy wheels" on the SX — via search summary of [KiaMedia 2013 Sorento features](https://KiaMedia.com/us/en/models/sorento/2013/features) and [Cars.com 2013 what's changed](https://www.cars.com/articles/2012/06/2013-kia-sorento-whats-changed).
- 19": J.D. Power lists 19" chrome-finished alloys on the 2013 Limited and also claims 19" machine-finished alloys on the SX, which conflicts with Kia's own 2013 SX 18" listing — [J.D. Power 2013 Sorento](https://www.jdpower.com/cars/2013/kia/sorento).
- Tire & Wheel Guide lists 2013 Sorento sizes as 235/65R17, 235/60R18, 235/55R19, 255/50R19, 255/45R20 (this appears to mix global markets; 255 sizes are not U.S. OEM per other sources) and gives 2.3 bar (33 psi) for 235/55R19 — [TireWheelGuide 2013 Sorento](https://tirewheelguide.com/sizes/kia/sorento/2013/).
- Pressure: TirePressure.com lists 33 psi front and rear for the 2013 model — [TirePressure.com](https://tirepressure.com/kia-sorento-tire-pressure); Wheel-size.com lists 33 psi for 235/55R19 on the 2015 Sorento — [Wheel-Size 2015 Sorento](https://www.wheel-size.com/size/kia/sorento/2015/); PureTyre (UK) gives 34 psi for 235/55R19 on 2010–2015 — [PureTyre](https://www.puretyre.co.uk/tyre-information/tyre-pressures/kia-tyre-pressures/kia-sorento-tyre-pressures-and-wheel-nut-torque-setting/). Door placard is authoritative; check cold.
- Kia owner's manuals say replacement tires must match the original size because ABS/ESC compare wheel speeds; mismatched sizes can keep ABS/ESC from working correctly; Kia also says replacement tires should match the factory load rating — [Kia owner's manual, Tire replacement](https://ownersmanual.kia.com/full_webhelp/LQ2/2027/en_US/topics/t00791.html); [Kia manual, Tires and wheels](https://ownersmanual.kia.com/docview/webhelp/doc/315418f9-1fb9-4c23-95a3-bc83ba8cd11f/topics/chapter9_7.html).
- Kia's owner's manual has a "Tire sidewall labeling" section explaining the size code (e.g., P235/65R17: P = passenger, 235 = section width mm, 65 = aspect ratio %, R = radial, 17 = rim diameter in), load index and speed symbol — [Kia owner's manual, Tire sidewall labeling](https://ownersmanual.kia.com/full_webhelp/CL4/2027/en_US/topics/t00710.html); 2014 Sorento manual pages on tire labeling — [ManualsDir 2014 Sorento manual](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/286099/kia-sorento-2014.html?page=460).
- DOT date code: the last four digits of the DOT/TIN on the sidewall are week + year of manufacture (e.g., "2419" = 24th week of 2019); first two digits are the week, last two the year; pre-2000 tires used three digits — [Edmunds, How old and dangerous are your tires](https://www.edmunds.com/car-care/how-old-and-dangerous-are-your-tires.html); [Arnold & Itkin, tire age DOT codes](https://www.arnolditkin.com/trucking-safety/mechanical-failures/wheels-tires-hubs/tire-age-dot-date-codes/).

### Inferences
- Practical size key for the owner: if the wheels are 17", buy 235/65R17 with load index >=103 and speed >=T; if 18", 235/60R18, load index >=102, speed >=H (an equivalent winter tire is commonly allowed a lower speed rating — confirm with the shop); if 19", 235/55R19 (load index to be read from the existing tire/placard).
- Since the FWD and AWD versions share the same OEM sizes in all sources found, drivetrain does not change size choice; it matters for whether all four must be replaced (section 3).
- The 2014 model year was a mid-cycle refresh, so "2014 Sorento" size listings (where SX got 19") should not be used for a 2013.

- Whether the 2013 SX ever came with 19" in the U.S. is contested (Kia/Autos.ca say 18"; J.D. Power says 19").

## 2. Are the tires truly due? Tread depth, winter minimums, coin tests, age, cracking

### Takeaway
NY inspection fails a tire below 2/32", but for snow/slush driving Tire Rack advises replacing around 5/32" (6/32" is where snow traction starts dropping) and 4/32" for wet roads. Tires older than about 6 years (automakers) to 10 years (tire makers' outer limit) should be replaced regardless of tread, so a set that is "old" and visibly worn going into a Columbia County winter is almost certainly due.

### Cited Findings
- NY inspection: tread depth must be at least 2/32" measured in two adjacent major grooves at the most worn point; DMV guidance says 2/32" or greater on all tires; spare not inspected — [NY DMV NYVIP](https://dmv.ny.gov/inspection/new-york-vehicle-inspection-program-nyvip); [NY DMV inspection brochure](https://dmv.ny.gov/brochure/new-york-state-vehicle-safetyemissions-inspection-program); [15 NYCRR 79.21 (Justia)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-15/chapter-i/subchapter-f/part-79/section-79-21/).
- Kia: tread wear indicator bars appear at 1.6 mm (1/16"), at which point the tire should be replaced — [Kia owner's manual, Tire replacement](https://ownersmanual.kia.com/full_webhelp/LQ2/2027/en_US/topics/t00791.html).
- Tire Rack: consider replacing at 4/32" if you expect wet conditions; snow traction reaches diminishing returns at about 5/32"; its chart labels 6/32" as "beginning to lose snow and slush winter traction"; use a quarter (Washington's head down; top of head = ~4/32") rather than a penny (Lincoln's head = 2/32") — [Tire Rack winter tips](https://www.tirerack.com/upgrade-garage/what-are-winter-tips); [Tire Rack tech 163](https://www.tirerack.com/tires/tiretech/techpage.jsp?techid=163); [Consumer Reports, enough tread for winter](https://www.consumerreports.org/tires/make-sure-your-tires-have-enough-tread-for-winter/).
- Tire Rack testing found 4/32" tires stop better in the wet than 2/32" tires but cannot match new tires — [Tire Rack tire test](https://www.tirerack.com/tires/tests/testDisplay.jsp?ttid=85).
- Tire age: no federal age limit; tire makers such as Continental and Michelin advise replacement no later than 10 years from manufacture with annual inspections after year 5; automakers such as Nissan and Mercedes-Benz recommend replacement at 6 years from production regardless of tread — [Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/car-care/how-old-and-dangerous-are-your-tires.html); [MoneyTalksNews, check these 4 digits](https://www.moneytalksnews.com/your-tires-can-look-fine-and-still-be-too-old-check-these-digits/).

### Inferences
- If the DOT code shows 2020 or earlier (6+ years old as of Nov 2026), replacement is justified on age alone under automaker-style guidance; if pre-2017, past the 10-year outer limit.
- Visible sidewall cracking ("dry rot"), bulges, or cords showing are replace-now conditions (general industry practice; no specific source fetched).
- For an Ancram winter with hills and unpaved town roads, aim for new tires (all-season with 3PMSF symbol or dedicated winter) rather than running anything near 4/32".

### Gaps
- Could not fetch NHTSA's own tire-aging page; "NHTSA recommends X years" claims in secondary sources were not verified, so none is attributed here.
- No specific source fetched on dry-rot/cracking criteria.

## 3. The slow leak, TPMS, repair costs, and whether to replace all four on AWD

### Takeaway
On a 13-year-old Sorento in salted Northeast roads, the usual slow-leak suspects are a puncture (nail/screw), bead-seat corrosion on the aluminum wheel, a leaking valve stem/TPMS valve core, or a bent/cracked wheel; a shop finds which with a dunk-tank/soapy-water test. A proper puncture repair (inside plug-patch) costs roughly $20–45; bead corrosion is fixed by cleaning the bead seat when tires are dismounted — which should be requested when new tires are mounted. Original TPMS sensors are well past typical 5–7 year battery life, so expect to replace some or all. Kia prefers replacing all four tires together.

### Cited Findings
- GM service bulletins (published via NHTSA) describe using a water dunk tank to locate a slow leak; if it is at the bead seat, the tire is dismounted and the bead seat inspected; corrosion looks like blistering of the wheel finish; other leak sources (tire body, valve stem, wheel flange) must also be checked — [NHTSA-hosted GM TSB 2019](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/MC-10169356-9999.pdf); [NHTSA-hosted GM TSB 2014](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2014/MC-10138631-9999.pdf); [VehicleServicePros, GM corroded wheel bead seat](https://www.vehicleservicepros.com/tsbs/article/53090897/gm-corroded-wheel-bead-seat-2012-04-02).
- GM's 2019 TSB repair: remove corrosion and flaking paint with a clean-up sanding disc; if total repairable corrosion exceeds 200 mm (8 in) linear, or reaches the wheel lip/face, replace the wheel — [NHTSA-hosted GM TSB 2019](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/MC-10169356-9999.pdf).
- Motor magazine: slow leaks and occasional flats from rim corrosion (aluminum oxide forming between bead and rim), commonly blamed on road salt — [Motor, Trouble Shooter Feb 2010](https://dev-www.motor.com/magazine-summary/trouble-shooter-february-2010/). Forum reports (anecdotal) describe this as a recurring northern-winter problem — [BITOG, bead sealing issues in northern winter](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/the-ongoing-bead-sealing-issues-in-northern-winter.351413/latest).
- Bead sealer can help but over-application can itself cause leaks (single forum poster, anecdotal) — [BITOG, leaking tire beads](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/leaking-tire-beads.337369/latest).
- Puncture repair: AAA describes a plug-and-patch combination as the only industry-accepted permanent repair for a tread puncture; tire must be dismounted to inspect inside — [AAA, Tire plug vs patch](https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/tire-plug-vs-patch-get-the-right-tire-repair). Max puncture size commonly cited as 1/4" (6 mm); shoulder and sidewall punctures not recommended for repair — [Tire Rack tech 77](https://www.tirerack.com/tires/tech/techpage.jsp?techid=77).
- Cost estimate: professional inside patch/plug about $20–45 per tire; plug-only $10–20 (temporary) — [Jerry, tire plug or patch cost](https://jerry.ai/car-repair/estimates/tire-plug-or-patch-repair-cost/) (estimate, aggregator).
- TPMS: sensor battery life ~5–7 years; batteries generally not replaceable (sensor replaced as a unit) — [Bruisers, Kia TPMS costs](https://bruisers.com/repairs/Tire%20Monitoring%20System/Kia/Rio); [TireGrades, TPMS battery replacement](https://tiregrades.com/tire-maintenance/pressure/tpms/tpms-battery-replacement); lifespan depends on battery type, temperature, usage — [Jalopnik](https://jalopnik.com/2211332/how-long-are-direct-tpms-sensors-designed-to-last/).
- TPMS cost estimates: $50–100 per sensor incl. labor (Bruisers); KBB general estimate $314–368 for TPMS sensor replacement (likely per job; scope unclear) — [KBB TPMS sensor replacement costs](https://www.kbb.com/service-repair-guide/tpms-sensor-replacement-costs); aftermarket sets of four for 2013 Sorento listed roughly $28–90 on marketplaces — [eBay listings](https://bo.ebay.com/b/Tire-Pressure-Monitors-Sensor-for-2013-Kia-Sorento/179696/bn_92985860). RepairPal's $272–290 figure is for the TPMS control module, not wheel sensors — [RepairPal 2013 Sorento](https://www.repairpal.com/cars/kia/sorento/2013).
- Kia owner's manuals: Kia recommends replacing all four tires at the same time; if not practical, replace front pair or rear pair; all four must be the same size for ABS/ESC — [Kia owner's manual, Tire replacement](https://ownersmanual.kia.com/full_webhelp/LQ2/2027/en_US/topics/t00791.html); [Kia manual chapter 9.15.4](https://ownersmanual.kia.com/docview/webhelp/doc/2d1c0d87-5b9a-4155-b717-5e85dad9f472/topics/chapter9_15_4.html).

### Inferences
- Because the tires are old and worn anyway, the slow-leaking tire need not be repaired separately unless the owner needs to drive on it until the new set is installed; the key step is asking the installer to dunk-test that wheel and clean/inspect the bead seat (and replace the valve stem/TPMS service kit) during mounting.
- Recommended ask at the shop: new valve stems or TPMS service kits (grommet, valve core, nut) on all four; test each TPMS sensor with a scan tool; replace dead sensors (budget estimate ~$50–100 each installed, per Bruisers; dealer OEM higher).
- On an AWD Sorento, buying four identical tires avoids tread-diameter mismatch strain on the AWD coupling; Kia's preference for four matches is consistent with that.

### Gaps
- No Kia document found specifying a maximum tread-depth difference (e.g., 2/32" or 3/32") for the 2013 Sorento AWD; that figure is an industry rule of thumb not verified here.
- No 2013-Sorento-specific TPMS battery life or local Columbia County installed price for TPMS sensors found.
- Could not verify any Sorento-specific TSB on wheel bead corrosion (evidence is from GM TSBs and general sources).

## 4. Winter driving conditions in Ancram / Columbia County

### Takeaway
Published snowfall averages for the area vary widely by source: a NOAA-normals-based county figure is about 31 in/yr, an Ancram-specific climate site says ~25 in, and commercial aggregators say 40–50 in. Ancram sits in the Taconic foothills of southeastern Columbia County, so higher elevations and hilly, partly unpaved town roads plus freezing rain make traction matter more than raw snow totals suggest.

### Cited Findings
- GeoStat: Columbia County about 31 in/yr, taken from the nearest NOAA 1991–2020 climate-normals station (station not named) — [GeoStat NY snowfall by county](https://www.geostat.org/NY/map/annual-snowfall).
- Weather-Atlas: Ancram averages about 24.65 in/yr over ~49.7 snowfall days, peaking in February (~5.6 in); data period/station not given — [Weather Atlas, Ancram climate](https://www.weather-atlas.com/en/new-york-usa/ancram-climate).
- USA.com lists Columbia County average annual snowfall at 41.54 in — [USA.com Columbia County weather](http://www.usa.com/columbia-county-ny-weather.htm); BestPlaces lists 50 in/yr for the county — [BestPlaces Columbia County climate](https://www.bestplaces.net/climate/county/new_york/columbia). Neither cites a NOAA period. (BestPlaces' 91 in figure for "Columbia, NY" is a different place — town of Columbia, Herkimer County — and should be ignored.)
- NYSkiBlog regional context: Atlantic influence keeps NYC-metro snowfall below ~35 in; snowfall increases toward the interior — [NYSkiBlog NY snowfall map](https://nyskiblog.com/directory/weather-data/new-york/state-average-snowfall-map/).

### Inferences
- A defensible statement: roughly 25–50 inches of snow per winter depending on source and elevation, with the NOAA-based value near 30 in for the county; hills toward the Taconic ridge (MA/CT border) likely see more.
- Rural Columbia County towns maintain gravel/dirt roads and use salt and sand on paved roads; this supports AWD + good winter tread and explains bead corrosion on alloy wheels (inference; no local DPW source fetched).

- No source retrieved on the share of unpaved town roads in Ancram or on town/county salt policy.

## 5. NY law: studded tires, winter tire requirement, chains

### Takeaway
New York permits studded tires only from October 16 through April 30. There is no statewide winter-tire or chain mandate for ordinary snow days; snow tires or chains are required only on designated snow emergency routes after a snow emergency has been declared.

### Cited Findings
- NY DMV (2016 release): studded snow tires may be used in NYS only from October 16 through April 30 — [LongIsland.com reprint of DMV release](https://www.longisland.com/news/10-14-16/dmv-nys-studded-snow-tires-permitted-beginning-october-16.html); same window reported by [WYRK](https://wyrk.com/new-york-studded-tire-removal-deadline/) and [Tire Rack studded tire laws](https://www.tirerack.com/winter/tech/techpage.jsp?techid=151). The window is in VTL §375 (attributed by secondary sources; statute text not read).
- Conflicting outliers: Modern Tire Dealer reports Oct 16–Apr 1 and WPDH reports Oct 1–Apr 30; majority and DMV say Oct 16–Apr 30 — [Modern Tire Dealer state guide](https://www.moderntiredealer.com/retail/article/55340250/studded-tires-a-state-by-state-guide); [WPDH](https://wpdh.com/these-tires-should-be-off-your-car-by-april-30-or-pay-a-fine/).
- VTL §145-c: tire chains and/or snow tires are required on a street or highway designated as a snow emergency route when a snow emergency has been declared by the state or municipality — [NY VTL §145-c (public.law)](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._vehicle_&_traffic_law_section_145-c); [FindLaw VTL 145-c](https://codes.findlaw.com/ny/vehicle-and-traffic-law/vat-sect-145-c/).
- VTL §145-d defines snow tires and protects radial-tire users from prosecution under local snow-tire ordinances during a declared snow emergency — [NY VTL §145-d (public.law)](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._vehicle_&_traffic_law_section_145-d).

### Inferences
- Studded tires are legal for the 2026-27 season starting Oct 16, 2026 and must come off by April 30, 2027; dedicated studless winter tires have no date restriction.
- Nearby Massachusetts (Great Barrington/Sheffield) and Connecticut have their own stud windows; not researched here.

### Gaps
- Did not read VTL §375 text directly or a current (2026) DMV page confirming no amendment.
- No verified source on any Columbia County or Town of Ancram snow-emergency route designations.
